Summary

The plan on one page

Couples in Sri Lanka still plan a wedding by asking relatives, scrolling Facebook pages and phoning vendors one by one. Prices are hidden, availability is a guess, and nothing is kept in one place. suba.lk becomes the one place where a couple plans, compares, books and invites.

Three bets

  • Vendors before visitors. A directory nobody lists in is useless. We sign up about 300 vendors in Colombo, Gampaha and Kandy before spending on couple marketing, starting with photographers, venues, decor, salons and wedding cars.
  • Free tools bring the traffic. The budget planner, the nakath (auspicious time) calendar, price guides and invitations are free. People find them on Google, TikTok and WhatsApp, and that traffic feeds the vendor pages that make money.
  • Every invitation is an advert. A wedding has about 300 guests, and each one opens a suba.lk link. Showing the couple's vendors on the invitation (with their permission) is our cheapest way to grow.

What we need to decide now

  • Company structure, who owns what and who does what between the two owners. Register a private limited company before signing vendor contracts and the PayHere merchant agreement.
  • Whether we take deposits (more revenue, but we handle refunds and disputes) or only pass leads to vendors at first. Our recommendation: leads and quotes first, deposits from month 4.
  • A budget for the first 6 months: about two people building, one person signing up vendors, and a small budget for ads and content.
Before any marketing: fix the 3 launch blockers in the current code: the hard-coded PayHere secret, uploads that are saved to the server's own disk, and missing rate limits on guest endpoints. Each is a few days' work at most.

Market and position

Why this can work in Sri Lanka

The size of the prize

Sri Lanka registers roughly 150,000 marriages a year (check the latest Registrar General figure before quoting it). A typical Colombo-area wedding costs between Rs. 1.5 and 6 million, mostly spent on venue, catering, photography, decor, dress and cars.

If suba.lk is involved in 10% of weddings and takes 2–3% of what they spend through fees and commission, that is a business worth several hundred million rupees a year. Getting there takes years, but the ceiling is high.

How couples plan today

  • Facebook and Instagram pages for each vendor, with prices only shared by DM.
  • General classifieds sites, and word of mouth through family and friends.
  • Phoning each vendor one by one to ask if the date is free.
  • Spreadsheets or notebooks for the budget. Printed cards for invitations.

Our position

Not just a directory, and not just an invitation tool. suba.lk is where the plan lives: the budget, bookings, guest list and invitations update each other. Directories can copy a vendor list. It is much harder to copy a couple's whole wedding plan.

Sinhala, Tamil and English from day one, with Buddhist, Hindu, Muslim and Christian wedding traditions covered properly. Most competitors are English-only and Colombo-only.

Traffic and growth

Becoming the top wedding site in the country

1. Search (Google): the main traffic source

  • A page for every category in every city: "wedding photographers in Kandy", "wedding cars in Galle", "Poruwa decorations in Negombo". With 25 cities, 16 categories and 3 languages, that is over 1,000 pages generated from real listings, each with prices and reviews.
  • The nakath calendar: "Poruwa nakath 2027" is searched heavily every year. A free calendar of auspicious times, checked by our partner astrologers, brings parents to the site, and it is the main entry point to the Traditions section.
  • A yearly price guide: "How much does a wedding photographer cost in Sri Lanka in 2027?", built from real vendor packages. News sites will link to it.
  • Pages in Sinhala and Tamil: there is far less competition for searches in these languages.

2. Growth built into the product

  • Each invitation reaches about 300 guests. A quiet "Made with suba.lk" line, plus credits for the couple's vendors on the invitation, can bring thousands of visits a month.
  • Vendors share their suba.lk profile and reviews on their own pages, which advertises us for free.
  • Shared budgets: the couple invites parents to view their budget plan, which brings in a second person.

3. Social media

  • TikTok and Instagram Reels: real weddings, "what it really costs" breakdowns, vendor portraits. Three posts a week.
  • Facebook groups where couples already plan. Answer questions there, don't spam.
  • A WhatsApp channel with nakath dates and wedding-season deals.

4. Vendor sign-ups (on the ground)

  • One full-time person signing up vendors, aiming for about 15 a day: visiting studios and salons, and meeting vendors at wedding fairs.
  • Founding vendor offer: Pro free for 6 months and a "Founding vendor" badge for the first 300 vendors.
  • We build their profile for them from their Facebook page in about 10 minutes, so they don't have to fill in anything.

5. Partnerships

  • Hotel banquet teams, bridal fairs, and banks (loans and card offers).
  • Sri Lankan community groups abroad, for couples planning a wedding back home.

What happens to the current app

The invitation editor, RSVP, seating, gallery and reminders stay as they are and become the Invite section of the couple's workspace. The current landing page is replaced by the hub home page. The marketing parts that break our design rules (sparkle icons, frosted-glass effects, about 570 hard-coded colours) are cleaned up at the same time.

Design direction

Calm, editorial and trustworthy, like a well-printed wedding programme. Photos of real work do the talking, and the layout stays out of the way.

The Traditions section works differently

Astrologers and nakath

Astrologers are checked before they're listed, and couples book a consultation with them (in person, by phone or by video), either to match horoscopes or to set the Poruwa time. The free nakath calendar is the main way people find this section. The tone stays respectful: no "lucky score" gimmicks.

Ashtaka and chanting

Each profile includes an audio sample, and couples can pick whether the ashtaka is performed in Sinhala or Pali. Booked together with Magul Bera drummers and Jayamangala Gatha groups. Most listings are in Sinhala, so these pages are written in Sinhala first.

Building it

Technical plan: build on what you already have

The current code (Next.js 16, Postgres with Drizzle, Better Auth, next-intl, PayHere) is a good foundation for a marketplace. We extend it rather than rewrite it. The invitation features stay as they are.

New database tables

TableWhat it stores
vendorsThe business profile, owner, city, verification status, plan
categoriesThe 16 services, with names in 3 languages
packagesName, price, what's included, in 3 languages
availabilityDates a vendor is blocked or booked
quote_requests / quotesRequests from couples and vendor replies
bookingsDeposit, status, refund state, PayHere order
reviewsLinked to a booking, so only real customers can review
weddings, budget_itemsThe couple's plan. Existing invitations link to it.
subscriptions, payoutsVendor plans and what we owe vendors
leadsPhone and WhatsApp taps, so we can show vendors what we sent them

Main technical choices

  • Search: start with the search built into Postgres (no extra service), then move to a dedicated search engine like Meilisearch once there are over 5,000 listings.
  • Photos: Cloudflare R2 storage with uploads sent straight from the browser, resized automatically. This also fixes the current problem of uploads being saved on the server's own disk.
  • Payments: PayHere for deposits and vendor plans. At first we pay vendors by weekly bank transfer and keep a record of every payment. Automatic splitting of payments can come later.
  • Messages: WhatsApp click-to-chat links at first, then the official WhatsApp Business API for quote alerts.
  • Search engines: pages are generated on the server, with structured data (LocalBusiness, AggregateRating, Offer) and a sitemap for each language.
  • Speed: category pages are cached and refreshed every hour, so they stay fast on 4G.
  • Admin panel: approve vendors, moderate reviews, handle disputes and refunds.

Fix before launch (from the code review)

  • Remove the hard-coded PayHere secret that's used when the setting is missing.
  • Move uploads to R2 and add rate limits.
  • Pin package versions and replace hard-coded colours with design tokens.

Risks and next steps

What could go wrong, and what to do this week

RiskWhat we do about it
Not enough vendors, so couples leaveLaunch city by city and category by category. Only advertise a category once it has at least 15 real vendors in that city.
Vendors take bookings outside suba.lkCharge mainly through plans and leads, not only commission. Give couples a reason to book on suba.lk: the deposit is protected, and only booked couples can leave reviews.
Deposit disputes and refundsA written cancellation policy for every booking, deposits held until the agreed date, and one person who handles disputes.
Fake reviews or fake vendorsOnly couples who booked can review. Vendors are verified (business registration or national ID, plus photos of real work).
Personal data (PDPA No. 9 of 2022)Collect as little as possible, let people export or delete their data, set retention periods, and get a short review from a local lawyer.
Wedding loans and financial rulesWe only refer couples. We never lend, take applications or hold borrowers' money. We sign a referral agreement with CBSL-licensed lenders only.
Sensitive traditional servicesUse respectful wording, no fortune-telling gimmicks, and write the Traditions content with astrologers and cultural advisors.
Two owners, unclear rolesWrite a shareholder agreement: who owns what share, who decides what, what happens if one owner leaves. Do this before taking money from anyone.
